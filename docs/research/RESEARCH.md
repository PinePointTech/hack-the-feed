## Corrections work
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Debunk videos on short video were effective; medical professionals got more engagement; high-tempo music helped (n=873) | Li, Li and Yang 2024, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication: [doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmae007](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmae007) |
| Corrections of about 30 seconds reduced misperceptions; experts and laypeople were about equally persuasive (n=956) | Heiss et al. 2024, "Debunking Mental Health Misperceptions in Short-Form Social Media Videos: An Experimental Test of Scientific Credibility Cues", Health Communication 39(13): 3059 to 3071. Summary: [Institute for PR](https://instituteforpr.org/should-experts-or-laypeople-debunk-misinformation-on-tiktok) |
| A 2026 review on AI and health misinformation argues for keeping humans in the loop | [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5tr3z1ps/qt5tr3z1ps.pdf) |
